# Mascot art direction

The first viewport pairs STRNK / “Powerful software” on the left with a muscular tree-stump mascot on the right. The mascot is a premium low-poly sculpture. Its primary read is a strong, flexing silhouette, then a tree stump, then a digital object.

## Shape and camera

- Use a frog’s-eye camera angle. Hide rear edges while preserving the silhouette.
- Use large readable polygon planes, 4–6 dominant roots, a compact heavy trunk, exaggerated shoulders/biceps, simplified fists, and subtle vertical trunk grooves.
- Avoid photorealism, cute cartoon styling, a person in a tree costume, a skirt of roots, or a default viewport-asset appearance.
- The studio camera sees the character's front. Fists face inward toward the trunk; viewer-left is the character's right hand. Thumb roots start near the wrist on the corresponding radial palm edge and wrap across the curled fingers. Fix a misplaced thumb through correct handedness, not by rotating the whole fist or reinterpreting the camera. Use a simple clenched mass and thumb, without detailed finger bars.

## Material and lighting

- Charcoal/dark brown, very rough, with little or no detailed bark texture. Geometry provides most surface detail.
- Let form and light lead: use only extremely subtle large-scale wood variation and slight per-face colour variation. Optional vertical grain must be broad and faint; omit tiny bark detail and gritty normal/bump textures.
- Reveal polygon planes through flat shading or controlled split normals, never painted triangle outlines or high-contrast random face colours.
- Neutral/warm key from upper-left, soft fill to keep shadow faces readable, and an orange rim/backlight primarily describing the silhouette.
- Background near `#10100f`. The mascot must remain readable at normal laptop brightness.
